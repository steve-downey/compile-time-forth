#+TITLE: Building a Compile-Time Forth in C++26 — Blog Series Index
#+AUTHOR: Steve Downey

* Overview

A working diary of building a Forth compiler and interpreter that runs
entirely inside the C++26 constant evaluator. The same compiled program is
meant to run at compile time and at runtime, and the reason I picked Forth is a
guess I am trying to make good on: that Forth's control words --- ~EXIT~,
~LEAVE~, ~CATCH/THROW~ --- are the one-shot, upward-only, dynamic-extent
discipline that sender/receiver structured concurrency already enforces.

These are entries, not chapters. Each was written as the work landed, so an
earlier one does not know what a later one found out. Where the plan met the
compiler and lost, I have left the loss in.

* Blog Posts

** [[file:post-0-the-pitch.org][Part 0 — The Pitch]]

Why build a Forth at compile time at all, what the pipeline is meant to look
like, and the one thing this is deliberately not: a stateful outer interpreter
with immediate words.

** [[file:post-1-standing-on-scheme.org][Part 1 — Standing on the Scheme Repo]]

The C++26 baseline, vendoring Beman Execution, and importing the ~foundation~
vocabulary by copy --- where a two-line equality fast path turned out to
compile on GCC and not on Clang.

** [[file:post-2-parser-combinators.org][Part 2 — Parser Combinators]]

Applicative combinators over immutable cursors, wired into the ~foundation~
typeclass machinery the Scheme reference left them disconnected from --- and the
one CPO that stopped being callable as a result.

** [[file:post-3-reading-forth.org][Part 3 — Reading Forth Text]]

The lexical layer (~-1~ is a number, ~1-~ is a word) and an arena-backed syntax
tree whose nodes hold each other by integer handle, closing the recursion
without a heap.

** [[file:post-4-grammar.org][Part 4 — The Grammar That Couldn't Be a Combinator]]

The plan said the combinator library /was/ the parser. Nested control structures
said otherwise, for a reason about types, not effort.

** [[file:post-5-the-machine.org][Part 5 — The Machine]]

Cells, two stacks, the dictionary, and the data space. Building the dictionary
before the data space had a real address type, and paying for it with a
placeholder.

** [[file:post-6-elaboration.org][Part 6 — Elaboration and the Effect Checker]]

Resolving words in program order, retyping that placeholder (and tripping a
warning doing it), and a stack-effect checker that is honest about the one thing
it cannot see across ~EXIT~.

** [[file:post-7-the-oracle.org][Part 7 — The Oracle]]

A direct evaluator to be the reference the compiled machine gets checked
against --- and the acceptance test that could not pass until I added two words
the plan forgot to list.

** [[file:post-8-survives-to-runtime.org][Part 8 — The Program That Survives to Runtime]]

Flattening the tree to a linear instruction array, back-patching branches, and
one ~constexpr~ value that runs the same at compile time and at runtime. Eight
opcodes of seventeen do real work; the rest are reserved on purpose.

** [[file:post-9-one-shot-api.org][Part 9 — The One-Shot API, and the Summit Not Yet Reached]]

~compiled_forth<"...">~ as the public surface, a hard compile error for a bad
program, and the honest accounting: the thesis from Part 0 is still not
executable, because the part that would prove it is not built.

** [[file:post-10-memory-words.org][Part 10 — The Address Was Always a Cell]]

~VARIABLE~/~CONSTANT~/~CREATE~ finally get something to read and write
through, in both backends at once --- and wiring it up means admitting this
Forth's memory was cell-granular from several steps back, with none of
Forth-2012's ~CELLS~/~CELL+~ words to say otherwise.

** [[file:post-11-counted-loops.org][Part 11 — The Two Cells the Checker Never Saw]]

~DO LOOP +LOOP I J LEAVE UNLOOP~ land in both backends, loop parameters and
all --- and the static checker turns out to have been wrong about ~DO~'s own
cost since two entries back, in a way only a nested loop could ever expose.

** [[file:post-12-the-patch-stream-was-already-there.org][Part 12 — The Patch Stream Was Already There]]

Part 0's refusal of Forth's outer interpreter doesn't survive a reread: the
back-patching it worried about was already the project's own target
representation. No code changed this entry --- only the argument for what has
to change next.

** [[file:post-13-in-is-not-a-cursor.org][Part 13 — >IN Is Not a Cursor]]

The Forth-2012 outer text interpreter, in interpret state only --- and a
design choice that pays off nowhere in this entry: ~>IN~ is a bare offset
sitting in the open, not a cursor buried inside the scanner.

** [[file:post-14-correct-by-accident.org][Part 14 — Correct by Accident]]

~:~ and ~;~ land, and with them a session image that outlives the constant
evaluation that built it --- and a first draft that passed every test for a
reason that had nothing to do with being right.

** [[file:post-15-the-cut.org][Part 15 — The Cut]]

Deleting the R1 pipeline outright --- reader, elaborator, direct evaluator,
batch codegen, close to 6300 lines --- and retargeting the public API onto
a session built once. What it costs: Part 7's oracle, the two evaluators
that had to agree, and nothing yet stands fully in its place.

** [[file:post-16-nothing-to-point-at.org][Part 16 — Nothing to Point At]]

~IF ELSE THEN~, ~BEGIN UNTIL~, and the whole ~DO LOOP~ family land as
immediate words on Forth-2012's own orig/dest discipline --- and a depth
check that looked obviously right gets caught wrong by one of my own tests
before it ever reached anyone else's code.

** [[file:post-17-something-to-point-at.org][Part 17 — Something to Point At]]

~'~, ~[']~, ~EXECUTE~, ~CREATE~, ~DOES>~, ~VALUE~/~TO~, and ~DEFER~/~IS~ all
get a real execution token --- and paying down a debt from Part 13 turns out
to be what makes any of it possible.

** [[file:post-18-what-in-was-for.org][Part 18 — What >IN Was For]]

~PARSE~, ~WORD~, ~CHAR~, ~S"~, ~."~, and ~ABORT"~ land, and a definition three
words long --- ~: ECHO-WORD 32 WORD COUNT TYPE ;~ --- reaches past its own
call for an argument that was never inside it. Two entries' worth of debt
about ~>IN~ finally cash out on the same example.

** [[file:post-19-the-gap-that-stopped-mattering.org][Part 19 — The Gap That Stopped Mattering]]

~CATCH~, ~THROW~, and ~ABORT~ land, and ~ABORT"~ stops being a hard stop and
becomes a real ~THROW -2~ --- but the entry is about what ~THROW~ had to do
to unwind a return stack holding an arbitrary mix of call frames, loop
frames, and stray ~>R~ values, and how that answers, without fully closing,
the teardown gap Part 11 admitted to eight entries back.

** [[file:post-20-just-edges.org][Part 20 — Just Edges]]

A real stack-effect checker returns, as an abstract interpreter over the
instructions a colon definition actually compiled instead of a tree that no
longer exists --- and Part 11's loop-teardown gap closes for free, because a
loop's back edge turns out to need the same agreement any other join does.
The first version of it was also wrong, caught not by a test I wrote but by
a conformance suite landing on the same tree the same week.

** [[file:post-21-the-oracle-is-not-an-authority.org][Part 21 — The Oracle Is Not an Authority]]

The other two legs of the oracle Part 15 promised finally exist: John
Hayes' own conformance tester, adapted as real Forth text and compiled by
this project's own interpreter, gating six batteries on a session's own
captured output; and a harness that runs the same programs through real
~gforth~ and diffs the stacks. One disagreement turns out not to be a bug
at all --- Forth-2012 leaves it ambiguous on purpose --- but two others
are, and neither gets fixed tonight.

** [[file:post-22-the-call-stack-was-the-continuation.org][Part 22 — The Call Stack Was the Continuation]]

A second executor lowers the same compiled instruction stream the machine
has run since Part 5 down to Execution26 senders, and agrees with it, block
by block, on every control construct that matters --- a call and a return
stop pushing an address and become an ordinary recursive C++ call, exactly
the refunctionalization Part 12 predicted. Two real bugs turned up along the
way, one of them sitting quietly in already-shipped code for two entries,
and the one place the theory doesn't reach --- the constant evaluator ---
stays open.
